# Product-owner review — Pool Forge

Reviewer: product owner, did not build it. Method: drove the running app at
`http://localhost:3001` with Playwright as a builder would, from sign-in to a
proposal a customer can open. Every finding below was reproduced in the browser;
code references are there to say *why*, not instead of evidence.

Session date: 2026-08-22. Branch `feat/world-class`, HEAD `33d5ce0` at the end of
the session (it moved twice while I was reviewing — see F1).

Reference docs read first: `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/build-priority.md`,
`docs/architecture.md`, `docs/world-class-assessment.md`, `docs/demo-findings.md`.

---

## Verdict

**I would not ship this.** Not because it is unfinished — plenty of unfinished
products ship — but because the two artifacts a builder's business runs on are
wrong in ways the app does not admit: **the link you send the customer shows a
different, much cheaper pool than the one you designed**, and **the proposal
prints "Heater: Included" and "Screen enclosure: Included" with $0 against
both.** A pool builder who sends one of these loses five figures on a single job
and blames the software, correctly.

The spine underneath is real and fast: nothing → priced proposal took **13 steps
and 27.6 seconds** driven at machine speed. That is genuinely good and worth
protecting. Fix the truth problems in the money path and this is a demo you can
put in front of a builder. Today it is not.

## 1. Blockers

### F1 — The customer share link renders an empty pool and a wrong price
**Severity: blocker**

*What I did.* Project "Timed Run": drew a 25×12 rectangle pool and a 770 sqft
concrete deck, ticked heater + salt, set 4 lights, pressed **Create link** on the
project page, then opened the resulting `/share/…` URL in a clean incognito
context — exactly what a customer does when they click the email.

*What I expected.* The same proposal the builder sees.

*What happened.* Two documents, same project, same minute:

| | Internal `/proposal` | Customer `/share/<token>` |
|---|---|---|
| Plan view | pool + deck drawn | **"No shapes drawn"** |
| Dimensions | 25 ft × 12 ft | **"Not specified"** |
| Surface area | 300 sqft | **0 sqft** |
| Line items | 5 | 2 |
| **TOTAL INVESTMENT** | **$45,514** | **$3,763** |

Reproduced on two separate projects (also $33,610 vs $3,286 on "PO Review
Residence"). The cause is one line: `src/app/share/[token]/page.tsx:46` calls
`loadDrawing(project.id).catch(() => ({ shapes: [] }))`, and `loadDrawing` starts
with `requireOrg()` → `auth()`. On a deliberately unauthenticated public page
that always throws, the `catch` swallows it, and the page confidently renders a
proposal for a pool that isn't there. It fails silently and looks fine.

*Why it matters commercially.* This is the only artifact that reaches the buyer.
It is also the artifact they legally accept — I typed "Dana Reyes" into **Accept
proposal** and the builder's project page now reads *"Accepted by Dana Reyes on
August 22, 2026."* against a $3,286 document with no pool in it. That is a signed
acceptance of the wrong scope at the wrong price.

### F2 — The proposal sells the heater and the screen enclosure for $0
**Severity: blocker**

*What I did.* Ticked "Include heater" and "Include screen enclosure" in the
**Selections** card (the card whose own subtitle says *"These drive the quote,
validation, and the customer proposal"*), saved, opened the proposal.

*What I expected.* A heater line and a screen line on the investment summary.

*What happened.* Under EQUIPMENT & FEATURES the document prints:

```
Heater             Included
Screen enclosure   Included
```

and the INVESTMENT SUMMARY contains no heater line and no screen line. Total
$45,514 for pool + deck + coping + pump + lights only.

Two independent causes:
1. The seeded price book has **5 items total** (`Pool Base — Wetted Area`,
   `Concrete Deck`, `Travertine Coping`, `Variable Speed Pump`, `LED Pool Light`).
   There is no heater item, no screen item, no spa item — so "Included" is free.
   I placed a **Spa** on the canvas and it likewise added $0 and shows "Spa $0"
   in the live quote breakdown.
2. Even with the right items, `src/modules/pricing/engine.ts:115-119` prices
   EQUIPMENT as `heaterSelected || saltSystemSelected ? 1 : 0`. Every EQUIPMENT
   item collapses onto one on/off switch: tick salt only and a heater line bills
   at qty 1; tick heater only and a salt cell bills at qty 1.
   `:122-125` prices SCREEN by **deck area**, which is not how a cage is measured.

*Why it matters commercially.* A 400k BTU heater is $4–6k installed; a screen
cage over a 25×12 pool in Florida is $12–25k. The document promises both and
charges nothing. This is not a missing feature — it is an affirmative false
statement on a contract-adjacent document, generated by default.

### F3 — Six of sixteen price-book categories silently price at zero
**Severity: blocker**

*What I did.* Opened **Price book → Add item** and read the category list.

*What I expected.* Every category I can create an item in to reach a quote.

*What happened.* The dialog offers 16 categories: `EARTHWORK, POOL, SPA, DECK,
COPING, EQUIPMENT, LIGHTING, SCREEN, BENCH, DRAIN, ELECTRICAL, WATER_FEATURE,
FENCE, WALL, LANAI, MISC`. `engine.ts:126-133` returns `quantity: 0` for
`LANAI, WATER_FEATURE, FENCE, WALL, ELECTRICAL, MISC` — and `computeQuote`
filters `quantity > 0`, so those lines **vanish from the quote with no warning**.

A builder who enters "Paver retaining wall — $9,400" (WALL), "Panel + bonding —
$2,800" (ELECTRICAL) or "Permit fees — $2,000" (MISC) sees them accepted, saved,
listed in the price book, and absent from every quote and every document.

*Why it matters commercially.* The price book is the one thing a builder is asked
to invest an afternoon setting up. A third of it is a hole. They will not find
out until a job is underbid.

## 2. Serious

### F4 — The editor's headline number contradicts its own breakdown
**Severity: serious**

*What I did.* Expanded the **LIVE QUOTE** dock in the editor.

*What happened.* On an **empty canvas** (0 sq ft, 0 LF, 0 gal, "No layers yet"):

```
LIVE QUOTE   $1,855
  Pool shell & finish   $0
  Spa                   $0
  Equipment             $1,750
  Deck & coping         $0
  Lighting & features   $0
  Subtotal              $1,750
  Permits & misc        $2,000
```

$1,750 + $2,000 = $3,750, and the header says $1,855. **"Permits & misc $2,000"
is a hardcoded constant** (`QuoteDock.tsx:35 const PERMITS_DEFAULT = 2000`,
rendered at `:171`) that is not in the subtotal, not in the total, not in the
price book, and not on any document. It exists only to make the panel look full.
The header is subtotal × 1.06 org tax.

Second contradiction in the same panel: with a pool drawn, "Deck & coping" showed
**$0** while $3,108 of Travertine Coping was in the quote — `classifySource()`
(`:52-67`) buckets by string-matching a `source` field that reads "Pool
perimeter", so coping lands in "Pool shell & finish".

*Why it matters.* A builder quoting in front of a customer reads the big number.
It is wrong twice: it excludes a $2,000 line printed directly beneath it, and its
category split misattributes real money.

### F5 — "Contribution to quote" is the whole quote, always
**Severity: serious**

*What I did.* Selected the rectangle pool. Then selected nothing.

*What happened.* Both states show **CONTRIBUTION TO QUOTE $30,358** listing
`Pool Base — Wetted Area $25,500 / Travertine Coping $3,108 / Variable Speed Pump
$1,750` — i.e. the entire job, attributed to whichever object you clicked, and
also to nothing. `QuoteContribution.tsx:26` says it out loud:
`const effective = matched.length > 0 ? matched : lines` — when nothing matches
(which is always, because the engine emits sources like `Pool surface area`, not
shape ids) it falls back to summing every line.

The card sits directly under a hint reading **"Widening to 16' adds +$3,160"** —
a hardcoded string with a `TODO` above it (`:63`), shown for every shape on every
project regardless of its actual width.

*Why it matters.* "What does this element cost?" is the core value proposition of
model-linked estimating. It currently answers with the job total and an invented
upsell.

### F6 — The editor's live quote is not live
**Severity: serious**

*What I did.* Changed selections on the project page (added 3 lights), saved, then
opened the editor.

*What happened.* Proposal computed **$33,610**. The editor's LIVE QUOTE showed
**$32,179** — off by $1,431 — and stayed at $32,179 through reloads. The editor
reads a cached quote refreshed only by `recomputeAndCacheEditor` inside the
drawing-save path (`EditorPersistence.tsx`), so a change made anywhere but the
canvas never invalidates it. In-session it is worse: I placed a pool and a deck
and the LIVE QUOTE did not move at all until after a save-and-reload.

*Why it matters.* Three different totals were visible in the product at one
moment ($32,179 editor header, $30,358 contribution card, $33,610 proposal). A
builder cannot tell which one to quote.

### F7 — The site plan is labelled "for permit submission" and would be rejected
**Severity: serious**

*What I did.* Opened **Site plan**.

*What happened.* Footer: *"SITE PLAN — FOR PERMIT SUBMISSION"*. The sheet
contains no property line, no house, no north arrow, no scale bar, no dimension
strings. `SETBACKS & EASEMENTS` reads `Front: — Side: — Rear: — Easements: —`.
`Jurisdiction: —`, `Parcel ID: —`. The plan-view box **overflows the sheet border
on the right**. The drawing itself is the same glossy blue customer render.

Worse, the app *has* setback numbers: the editor inspector shows
`From house 26' 5" — south face` and `Setback 19' 11" right (req. 5')` for the
same pool. Those numbers are measured against an invisible house hardcoded at
`measurements/engine.ts:99: DEFAULT_HOUSE_Y_IN = -28 * 12` — a wall the user
cannot see, move or place. So the app invents setbacks on screen and prints
dashes on the document that legally needs them.

*Why it matters.* Permit turnaround is the builder's schedule. A packet that
comes back rejected costs weeks, and the customer blames the builder.

### F8 — Screen-enclosure RFQ quotes a 0 sqft cage, and contradicts itself on pricing
**Severity: serious**

*What I did.* Opened **Screen enclosure RFQ** with "Include screen enclosure"
ticked.

*What happened.* `Coverage: 0.0 sqft (deck)`, `Height: —`, `Option: —`,
`Type: Standard screening`. No panel schedule, no post spacing, no beam spans, no
wind-load or Florida Product Approval reference. A cage vendor cannot quote from
this. The page also carries two buttons — **"Show internal pricing"** and **"Show
retail subtotal"** — directly above the line *"Pricing intentionally omitted —
this is a request for quote."*

*Why it matters.* `docs/world-class-assessment.md` names screen enclosures as the
beachhead wedge. This is the wedge document and it is not usable by the vendor
it's addressed to.

### F9 — Material selection is a no-op that quotes prices it never charges
**Severity: serious**

*What I did.* Selected the pool, opened INTERIOR FINISH, changed "Pool Water" to
"Glass Mosaic — Aqua mix". Reloaded.

*What happened.* The inspector reverted to "Pool Water". The quote did not move
($32,179 before and after). Confirmed as designed —
`ClientCommandHandlers.tsx` echoes the input for `set.shape.material` /
`pool.material.set` and persists nothing.

Compounding it, the **Materials** panel lists ten materials with real unit prices
— `PebbleTec — Cobalt $7.10/sqft`, `White Plaster $4.25/sqft`,
`Travertine — Ivory $28.00/lf`, `Paver Deck — Tan $14.00/sqft` — **none of which
exist in the price book**. The pool is billed at `Pool Base — Wetted Area
$85.00/sqft` whichever finish you pick. Two price lists, one of them decorative.
And the default interior finish is "Pool Water"; the picker also offers a
$15.00/**lf** tile band as a **sqft** interior finish.

*Why it matters.* Finish selection is where the upsell lives. It is also the
thing a customer changes three times during a sit-down. Right now every one of
those changes is theatre.

### F10 — Two of the ten toolbar shortcuts don't work, and ⌘K does nothing
**Severity: serious**

*What I did.* Clicked the canvas, then pressed each advertised single-key
shortcut and read back which tool armed.

*What happened.*

```
w → Water feature ✓   l → Lights ✓   d → Deck ✓   m → Measure ✓
t → Annotation ✓      c → Comment ✓  v → Move ✓
r → Move  ✗ (should arm Pool shape)
s → Move  ✗ (should arm Steps & shelves)
```

The toolbar prints "R" and "S" under those two buttons. They are the two tools
you reach for first.

Separately, the editor's own empty state says **"No layers yet. Drop a stencil or
use ⌘K → Add to start."** ⌘K opens nothing. The palette exists but only via the
pink toolbar button. And there is no drag-to-place from the stencil panel, so
"Drop a stencil" describes an interaction that does not exist either. The empty
state gives two instructions and both are wrong.

### F11 — First edit of a session was thrown away (fixed mid-review)
**Severity: blocker at the time of testing; verify it stays fixed**

*What I did.* Opened a fresh project's editor, placed one rectangle pool, waited
25 seconds (well past the 800 ms debounce), confirmed `LAYERS 1 · Standard
rectangle · 25' × 12'`, then reloaded.

*What happened.* `LAYERS 0 · "No layers yet."` Surface area 0. Zero server-action
POSTs fired. Placing a **second** shape saved **both**. Confirmed in Postgres:
`Drawing.rootJson->shapes` length 0. I then lost a 35×22 concrete deck the same
way in a later session without noticing until the quote came back short.

This was fixed at commit `33d5ce0` while I was writing, and I re-verified: one
edit + reload now persists, and the header shows "Saved 1s ago". Recording it
anyway because it is the exact failure mode that kills trust — *every edit after
the first saved*, so a builder cannot tell which half of their afternoon is real.

## 3. Incoherent — two ways to do one thing

### F12 — The project form has a dead vocabulary and a live one, side by side
**Severity: serious**

The project page shows a **Pool** card with eleven free-text boxes — Pool type,
Depth (shallow), Depth (deep), Interior finish, Equipment package, Sanitization
package, **Heater selection**, Lighting selection, Deck material, Coping material,
**Screen option** — and immediately beneath it a **Selections** card with
**Include heater**, **Include salt system**, **Include screen enclosure**, **Pool
lights (qty)** subtitled *"These drive the quote, validation, and the customer
proposal."*

So the app asks about the heater twice. Typing "Pentair MasterTemp 400" into
*Heater selection* changes no price, no validation, and no line item; only the
checkbox does. `pool-fields.ts` states this plainly in a comment — descriptive
strings are display-only — but nothing in the UI says so. A builder will fill the
detailed field, skip the checkbox, and ship a quote with no equipment on it.

Same duplication on depth: I typed `3-6` / `6-0` into the project form; the
proposal printed **"3 ft shallow / 5 ft deep"** from the canvas geometry instead,
and the validation drawer simultaneously raised the error **"Set both shallow and
deep end depths"** for a pool whose inspector already reads `SH 3.0 / DP 5.0`.
Three depth values, one pool.

Same on sanitization: "Include salt system" prices, "Sanitization package" prints
— and the proposal's `Sanitization` row rendered **blank** on a project with salt
switched on.

### F13 — Two different controls both called "Plan"
**Severity: annoyance**

Top-centre: `Plan | Design | Build | Customer` (presentation modes). Bottom-left:
`Plan | 3D | Section` (camera/view). Both render a button whose visible text is
"Plan", meaning different things. Playwright refuses the click as ambiguous,
which is a fair proxy for a user hesitating. The bottom-left switcher is also
clipped by the viewport edge — "Plan" is half-hidden behind the corner.

### F14 — The Pool tool now offers things that aren't pools
**Severity: annoyance**

`docs/demo-findings.md` records the picker being fixed from 4 shapes to all 17
from the catalogue. It overshot: the **Pool shape** dropdown now lists *Standard
steps, One step, Step sets, Corner steps, Square steps,* and *Spa* — which is
what the separate **Steps & shelves** toolbar tool is for. Two tools, overlapping
menus.

### F15 — "Coming soon" sits next to the working version of the same feature
**Severity: annoyance**

Header buttons carry `aria-label="Comments (coming soon)"` and
`aria-label="Sun study (coming soon)"`. Meanwhile a working **SUN STUDY** slider
(12:00p, sunrise 6:21A, sunset 8:31P) is pinned to the bottom-left of the canvas,
and a second **Comments** button sits in the right panel. The palette also
advertises `⌘E` / `⌘⇧E` for exports on a build where ⌘K is unbound.

## 4. The app claiming success without doing anything

### F16 — "Hardcoded for demo" is shipped user-facing text
**Severity: serious**

With nothing selected, the inspector header reads:

```
Project overview
Hardcoded for demo
```

`SelectionCard.tsx:53`. This is on screen every time the editor opens, above the
quote figures. Any prospect who sees the app sees it.

### F17 — Image import declares every field reviewed before anything is read
**Severity: serious**

*What I did.* Project → **Import from image** → *Start an import* → uploaded a
600×400 PNG → pressed **Analyze image**, waited 30 s.

*What happened.* Top-right, from the moment the screen loads and still after
analysis: a green tick and **"Every low-confidence field has been reviewed."**
Meanwhile the same screen reads `EXTRACTION 0 of 3 stages`, `Classify NOT RUN`,
`Extract NOT RUN`, `Calibrate NOT RUN`, and all 15 fields read **"Not read /
Not scored"**.

After Analyze: `1 of 3 stages`, Classify **DONE**, Extract and Calibrate still
NOT RUN, no field populated, no error, no explanation of why the pipeline
stopped, and the button relabels itself to **"Re-analyze"** as if it had
finished. The red banner *"This image has no scale, so no geometry can be
applied"* and the disabled **Apply to project** are both honest and good — the
green tick above them is not.

*Why it matters.* `docs/build-priority.md` calls image ingestion the wave that
"jumped the queue" and `docs/competitive-analysis.md:85` calls AI the uncontested
gap. If this is the differentiator, a green checkmark over an empty extraction is
the worst possible first impression.

### F18 — Accepting a proposal doesn't move the project
**Severity: serious**

The customer accepted; the project page shows *"Accepted by Dana Reyes on August
22, 2026."* The status dropdown still reads **Draft**. The app defines a pipeline
— Draft → Ready for review → Proposal sent → Approved → Construction ready — and
a signature moves the project through none of it. No email, no dashboard badge,
nothing. The builder finds out by chance.

### F19 — Buttons in the quote dock have no handlers
**Severity: annoyance**

**Compare options** and **Generate proposal** in the expanded LIVE QUOTE dock
(`QuoteDock.tsx:175-181`) are `<Button>`s with no `onClick`. "Generate proposal"
is the highest-intent button in the editor and it is inert — which also breaks
the repo's own non-negotiable: *every user-driven action dispatches through the
command registry*.

### F20 — Internal field keys are shown to the user
**Severity: annoyance**

The validation drawer and command palette print raw schema paths under every
item: `POOL · DEPTHSHALLOW`, `PROJECT · CUSTOMERNAME`,
`EQUIPMENT · EQUIPMENTPACKAGE`, `EXPORT · PROPOSALEXPIRESAT`. The construction
packet prints `Project ID: cmt52jx17001fsbupe6prsr99`. Both the global and repo
conventions say user-facing messages never expose internal ids.

Worse, two of those nags are wrong: *"No equipment package selected — Pick an
equipment package (includes the pump)"* points at a free-text field that prices
nothing, and the pump is already `required` on every quote regardless. *"Try
PebbleTec Cobalt finish — switch the interior finish to PebbleTec Cobalt and
recompute the quote"* recommends an action that, per F9, persists nothing and
recomputes nothing.

## 5. Missing table stakes

### F21 — The proposal is not a document a builder can send
**Severity: blocker for sale, serious for the build**

Judged as the deliverable, not as a page. Absent:

- **No signature or acceptance block.** The site plan has three signature boxes
  and the construction packet has two; the customer proposal — the one that gets
  signed — has none.
- **No payment schedule, deposit, or draw schedule.** Every pool contract has one.
- **No company address, phone, or contractor licence number.** Florida requires
  the CPC licence number on the contract. **Company settings** holds exactly four
  fields — company name, logo URL, brand colour, sales tax — with nowhere to put
  any of it.
- **No expiration date printed**, while the terms paragraph says *"Pricing valid
  until the proposal expiration date listed above."* It is not listed above. The
  legal text refers to a date the document does not contain.
- **No 3D render.** The app's best asset renders a real pool in the viewport and
  the proposal ships flat SVG rectangles. `docs/world-class-assessment.md` flagged
  this a month ago; it is unchanged.
- **No scope-of-work narrative, no job-specific exclusions, no options/alternates,
  no financing.**
- "Proposal #: **E6PRSR99**" is the tail of the database cuid, not a number a
  builder can reference on the phone.
- Export is `window.print()`. No stored PDF artifact, so there is no record of
  what was sent.

### F22 — The construction packet is a customer render with a spec table stapled on
**Severity: serious**

CONSTRUCTION LAYOUT is the identical glossy blue gradient pool from the customer
proposal. No dimension strings, no centrelines, no equipment pad, no plumbing
runs, no sections, no elevations, no steel schedule. It ships a **SYMBOL LEGEND**
listing "Equipment pad · Access arrow · Property line · Setback line · Center
line · Dimension line · Approval block · Construction notes block" — eight
symbols, none of which appear on the drawing.

Ironically the editor *does* have construction content: **Build** mode overlays
`#3 REBAR · 18" OC` and `GAS LINE · 3/4"` on the model. None of it reaches the
packet. (The `GAS LINE` callout is also half-hidden behind the sun-study widget.)

Fields printed as spec that are actually hardcoded defaults: `Spa shape: Square
(default)`, `Material: Phifer SunScreen (default)`.

### F23 — Company settings can't hold a company
**Severity: serious**

Four fields. No address, no phone, no licence, no default markup, no proposal
terms, no team/users, no payment terms, no logo *upload* (a URL field only — and
`docs/build-priority.md` has no item for it). A builder cannot configure their own
business enough to send one legal document.

### F24 — The dashboard tells a builder nothing about their business
**Severity: annoyance**

Sixteen identical cards, each: name, a status dropdown, "No customer", "Updated
8/22/2026". No quote value, no thumbnail, no address, no search, no sort, no
totals, no pipeline value by stage. The status filter chips exist but every
project is Draft. There is nothing here a builder would open in the morning.
(The dev database is also full of harness leftovers — `E2E mprwlf`, `Moves
0a7zbe`, `Ballast Voice`, `LSDKAJFLKASDJF` — which is a seeding hygiene issue,
not a product one, but it is what the demo shows.)

### F25 — No form control on the project page has an id, name, or label association
**Severity: annoyance**

All 26 inputs/selects/textareas on `/projects/[id]` return `id=""`, `name=""`,
`aria-label=null`, and no `<label for>`. Clicking a label does not focus its
field, screen readers announce nothing, browser autofill cannot fill the customer
name/email/phone/address block, and there is no progressive-enhancement form
post. (The login page does this correctly — `#email`, `#password` — so it is
inconsistency, not ignorance.)

## 6. Built but pointless, or built out of order

### F26 — Site grading exists, prices nothing, and hides the pool
**Severity: serious**

`Grade → Site grading [On]` produces a panel with `Datum (ft) 0`, `0 elevations`,
`Cut 0 yd³`, `Fill 0 yd³`, `Fall across site 0 ft`, `Steepest slope 0%`. There is
no EARTHWORK item in the price book, so cut/fill can never reach a quote even
once elevations are entered. Meanwhile switching grading on **made the pool
disappear from the 3D scene** — the water surface and shell vanished, leaving the
coping frame floating on khaki terrain, and the spa lost its water. A feature that
prices nothing and breaks the render is negative value today.

### F27 — 48 documented tools that don't exist
**Severity: annoyance**

`/docs/tools` opens with *"11 tools you can use today, and 48 designed but not
built."* Honest, and I'd rather have it than not — but a customer-visible
reference catalogue that is 81% aspirational, shipped before the price book can
hold a heater, is effort in the wrong place.

### F28 — Presentation modes are near-invisible
**Severity: annoyance**

**Customer** mode produced a screenshot indistinguishable from **Design** on my
design. **Build** mode does something real (rebar/gas overlays). Three of the four
top-level modes are the most prominent control in the editor and two of them
appear to do nothing to a normal drawing.

## What is genuinely good (so it doesn't get broken)

Short, because the list above is the point — but these are the assets worth
defending:

- **13 steps, 27.6 seconds** from sign-in to a rendered proposal, warm. Compared
  with a desktop CAD seat plus a separate estimating stack, that is the pitch.
- **Measurements are correct and instant.** 25×12 → 300 sqft, 74 LF, 596 sqft
  wetted, 8,977 gal, recomputed on every change.
- **The validation drawer is real** — 2 errors / 5 warnings / 5 passes, each with
  a plain-English fix and a jump target. It is the most trustworthy panel in the
  app.
- **The import review wizard's honest failure states** — "This image has no
  scale, so no geometry can be applied", a disabled Apply button, and
  "One undoable action. Nothing is written until you press this." That is the
  right voice. Use it everywhere else.
- **The proposal's typography** is better than most builder software.

## What I would fix, in order

1. **F1** — public share page must load the drawing without a session. One line;
   it is the difference between a demo and a liability.
2. **F2 / F3** — never print "Included" against a $0 line; refuse to export, or
   print "Priced separately". Then fix the EQUIPMENT one-switch bug and either
   wire the six dead categories or remove them from the Add-item dialog.
3. **F12** — delete the eleven dead free-text fields or make them the only
   fields. Two vocabularies for the same pool is what generated most of this list.
4. **F4 / F5 / F6** — one quote number, computed in one place, shown everywhere.
   Delete `PERMITS_DEFAULT` and the hardcoded "+$3,160" hint.
5. **F16 / F17 / F19** — remove "Hardcoded for demo", stop the green tick over an
   empty extraction, wire or hide "Generate proposal".
6. **F21 / F23** — signature block, payment schedule, licence number, expiry date.
   These are why a builder pays.

Everything else can wait for a customer to complain about it.
